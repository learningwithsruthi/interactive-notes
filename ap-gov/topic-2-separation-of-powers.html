Separation of Powers

The three branches, what each one does, and every way one branch can stop another.

MapWhat this topic covers

The framers had one big fear: any single group holding all the power. Their solution has two parts that students often mix up. Separation of powers splits the work into three branches. Checks and balances gives each branch a way to stop the others. You need both words, and you need to know the difference.

2.1Separation of powers

Separation of powers: the work of government is split among three branches so that no one branch is too powerful. Each branch holds powers the others cannot take.

ArticleBranchWhoMain job
Article ILegislativeCongress (House + Senate)Makes the laws
Article IIExecutivePresident, Vice President, Cabinet, federal agenciesEnforces the laws
Article IIIJudicialSupreme Court and lower federal courtsInterprets the laws

Why is Congress listed first? The framers expected lawmaking to be the most powerful function of government. That is also why Congress is the branch they split in two (House and Senate): it was the branch they worried about most.

Do not mix these up Separation of powers = dividing the jobs. Checks and balances = each branch can block or limit the others. A question about the president vetoing a bill is about checks and balances. A question about why Congress writes laws and the president enforces them is about separation of powers.

2.4Why it was built this way

James Madison explained the logic in Federalist 51, another required document. People who hold power will always want more. So instead of hoping leaders are good, build a system where each branch's self-interest makes it guard against the others.

"Ambition must be made to counteract ambition."Federalist 51, James Madison (1788)
"If men were angels, no government would be necessary."Federalist 51

Connect it to Topics 1 and 3: Federalist 70 says make the executive strong. Federalist 51 says make every branch able to push back. Those are not contradictions. A strong president facing a strong Congress and independent courts is exactly the balance the framers wanted. The cost is that government is slow on purpose, which is why gridlock is a built-in feature, not a bug.
